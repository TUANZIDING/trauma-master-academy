# Trauma Skills Academy V1 content standard

## Purpose

The first release candidate contains nine high-value trauma skills for early clinicians. It is a supervised simulation curriculum, not a bedside instruction set and not a substitute for ATLS, DAS, WHO BEC, AIUM, AO, ACS, or local credentialing.

## Standard page structure

1. Competency objective: knowledge, teamwork, observable performance, effect check, and reassessment.
2. Evidence locator: source ID, version/date, topic, population/setting, and rights boundary.
3. Five-station route: recognise, prepare, simulate, check, and reassess.
4. Visual package: concept image, supervised skill image, and monitoring/debrief image.
5. Synthetic case: observations and team report; no real-patient data.
6. Formative OSCE: domain-based instructor observation before any calibrated score.

## Video production

- concept sequence: clinical problem and learning question;
- team-preparation sequence: roles, cognitive aids, infection prevention, monitoring, and equipment category;
- supervised-simulation sequence: trainer-only performance with an instructor visible or explicitly stated;
- effect-check sequence: observable response, residual concern, documentation, and next reassessment;
- debrief sequence: error trap, communication gap, and transfer of learning.

Every video must display the simulation status and must avoid filming identifiable patients.

## Evidence rules

- ATLS public pages support curriculum framing but do not replace licensed manual topic review.
- Topic-specific claims use the relevant public guideline or practice parameter.
- A source from resuscitation, anaesthesia, prehospital care, ultrasound, orthopaedics, or public bleeding-control training is labelled with its original setting.
- Different guidelines are not forced into artificial agreement; technique variation is surfaced for expert review.
- Source presence is not equivalent to clinical approval.
